Sublevel

A one-on-one card duel played in a basement. Both fighters pick a move in secret, both reveal at the same instant, and a contract on Monad resolves the turn. There are no dice and no referee to trust. The cards are NFTs, and their fight records are written by the rules and by nothing else.

01 / OverviewWhat it is

Sublevel is a competitive duel game with a fight-club look: photocopied posters, tape, a bare bulb over a table. Two players each bring a small corner of fighters and one-shot dirty tricks. Every turn each side secretly chooses one action, locks it in, and the two choices are revealed together. The result depends only on what you picked against what they picked.

It is built for the Monad Blitz Pune hackathon. The brief asked for a serious game product where the Web3 part is meaningful, with polish and originality. It draws on two reference projects without copying either. From Promptmon it takes the idea of fighters you author yourself and cards worth owning. From Street Aura it takes the live, block-driven feel of a match on a fast chain. Its own ideas are the simultaneous-move duel, the guard triangle, and a hidden-hand format that works on a public ledger.

One bout, from the player's seat

  1. Build or pick a corner. House fighters, or your own from the Workbench: one style, one light and one heavy attack, one spread.
  2. Challenge or accept. A pool of cards is locked into the bout. Neither side can swap a card mid-fight.
  3. Lead. Each side secretly picks which fighter starts. Both are revealed together.
  4. Turns. Each side locks one action as a hash. Once both are locked, both reveal. The contract resolves the turn and emits what happened.
  5. Finish. Knock out the target number of fighters, or win on a decision at the turn cap. Each card that fought gets a permanent entry in its record.

02 / RulesThe game

A fighter is five choices

A fighter is a style, one light attack, one heavy attack of that style, and a spread. That gives 78 builds. The rule is enforced at mint, so a card can never exist in a form the rules do not understand. A prompt or a name changes how a fighter looks and what their story is. It never changes what they can do.

The style triangle scales damage by 1.5 (rounded up) when you hold the advantage and by two thirds (minimum 1) when you do not. Pierce attacks ignore the bad matchup.

Actions and Grit

Eight actions exist: Attack 1, Attack 2, Brace, Switch to a bench slot, and Use a trick. Everything except Brace costs Grit. Grit is gained at one per turn and capped at five, so a heavy attack is a decision about when you can afford it. A light attack costs two or less, a heavy attack three or more.

How a turn resolves

  1. Pay costsGrit is spent for attacks, switches and tricks.
  2. SwitchFighters change places.
  3. TricksHeals and denial first, then damage and nullifiers.
  4. AttacksBoth attacks land at the same time.
  5. BraceA guard gives +1 Grit, but only the first in a row.
  6. RefundA Switch no attack punished gets its cost back.
  7. KOs, Grit, endKnockouts, Grit gain, then end checks.

The guard triangle

A second triangle sits under the first, between attacks and Brace.

Endings. Reach the knockout target to win. If both sides would win on the same turn, the surviving fighters re-enter at half HP for sudden death, where the next knockout wins. At the turn cap a decision compares knockouts, then remaining HP, then damage dealt.

Tricks are one-shot cards: Cheap Shot (2 damage, ignores Brace), Patch Up (heal 3), Second Wind (+2 Grit), Smoke Bomb (their attack deals half), Sucker Punch (if they attack, take nothing and deal 3), Low Blow (they lose 2 Grit).

03 / Design validationIs it deep?

The first question was whether a simultaneous-move duel stays interesting after the first few games. The rules were tested before any contract was written. A simulator plays millions of turns, a value function is fitted to real game states, a regret-matching solver finds near-equilibrium play, and an exploit finder checks whether a habit can be punished.

That process changed the game. An early version let Brace dominate. The matrix and the fitted evaluation showed it, and the guard triangle plus the free-Switch refund came out of that finding. The attack menu was cut to one light and one heavy per fighter for the same reason: it removes trap builds and keeps the guard triangle alive in every fight.

Stated plainlyThe gate passed with caveats, and they are written down in packages/engine/RULES.md. One requirement was restated after testing (R1 became R1′). The Lite reading-skill score is 7.3 against a target of 8. Full's position-shift measure is low. Sample sizes are modest and should be rerun at n ≥ 400 before launch.

04 / The chain's jobWhy Monad

The test for every on-chain piece is simple: what breaks if this were a database? For this game the answers are specific.

On-chainIf it were a database
Commit and reveal, then resolution by the rulesThe operator could read a move before it is revealed or edit a result.
Hidden-hand slot hashes and openings (Full)No proof that nobody swapped a card mid-bout.
Card records and scarsHistory becomes editable, so a career means nothing.
Locks while a pool is in a boutYou would need a trusted custodian.
A frozen, versioned rulebookThe operator could quietly rebalance a card you own.

Monad is the right place because the game needs a transaction per turn per player. A Lite bout takes roughly 12 to 16 transactions and a Full bout about 32. At about 0.3 second blocks (documented slots are 300 ms with 600 ms finality, and testnet blocks measured near 318 ms) that cadence feels like a game and not like waiting on a ledger. Session keys remove the wallet pop-up from every move.

Rules

Engine

A pure library, line for line the same rules as the TypeScript engine. No randomness, no time, no storage. Bouts are stored as 88 packed 16-bit values (176 bytes).

NFTs

Cards

ERC-721 with ERC-4906. The build, name and look are immutable. The career record is written only by the Arena. Minting is free, names use a safe alphabet, and tokenURI is generated on-chain.

Pools

Armory

Registers pools (Lite: 3 cards, Full: 8 cards with at least 3 fighters and 2 tricks) and locks them. Owns the session keys.

Referee

Arena

Challenges, secret simultaneous moves, a 90-second phase clock, forfeits, hidden-hand openings, and settlement.

Deploy

Bootstrap

One transaction deploys and wires all three, then has no function left. Nobody, including the sender, holds any power afterwards.

The fairness rules

  • A commit is keccak(chainId, arena, boutId, turn, phaseTag, player, choice, salt). The phase tag keeps lead, action and replacement commits apart, and each (side, tag, turn) is used at most once per bout.
  • A reveal that does not match its commit, or that is illegal, forfeits the bout. A missed deadline forfeits. If both sides miss, the bout is voided.
  • A session key can commit, reveal and open cards for the owner who authorised it. It cannot create or accept challenges, register pools, transfer, approve or lock anything. It is authorised per owner for up to 7 days, so keys should be short-lived.
  • No randomness anywhere, and no function handles MON. There is no custody or reentrancy surface to attack.
  • Records are labelled unranked in v1. Open challenges can be farmed with spare wallets, and ranked play needs blind matchmaking.

06 / The hard partFull: hidden hands

A public ledger cannot hide a card by default. Full needs your bench and tricks to stay secret until played, while still proving nobody cheated. The pool of 8 cards is public, so a plain hash of a card could be guessed by trying all 8.

  1. Blinded slots. The lead commit binds the lead slot and five slot hashes, one per hidden slot. Each hash includes its own secret salt, so there is nothing to brute-force.
  2. Open on demand. A card is revealed in the same transaction as the move that first needs it: the lead, a switch, a trick, or a replacement.
  3. Every opening is checked. It must match its locked slot hash, belong to the locked pool, fit the slot (fighter or trick), and not repeat a card. A bad opening forfeits.
  4. Almost nothing else is forced. Only two rules read unplayed fighters: a tied turn-cap decision, which compares every fighter's HP, and sudden-death re-entry. The engine pauses there. Both sides open exactly what is needed, or forfeit at the deadline. Tricks are never forced.
Secrets must survive a refreshA Full bout needs six secrets for its whole length. The client derives all of them from one wallet signature over a per-bout message, so a player who reloads the page signs again and carries on. This is tested: both seats were rebuilt from fresh signatures mid-bout and the bout finished correctly.

07 / What you seeThe client

View

The Table

A side-on 3D diorama built with React Three Fiber: concrete wall, worn floor, wooden table, fighter standees under a work light. The same game state also drives a flat 2D view, so the game never depends on the 3D layer.

Build

Workbench

Assemble a fighter from modules with a live poster and a fight sheet derived from the rulebook. Names follow the same alphabet the Cards contract enforces. Saved fighters live in the browser.

Learn

Manual

An in-game rulebook and a standalone HOW_TO_PLAY.md. Its tables are generated from the rulebook, so the text cannot drift from the rules.

Art is procedural: SVG portraits, canvas posters, a procedural WebAudio soundscape, and public-domain Poly Haven textures (licences recorded in ASSETS.md). The client is Next.js 16 with React 19. A TypeScript chain library (viem) holds every commitment, the secret derivation, and a Seat class that performs each on-chain call.

Known limits

  • Gas figures are priced for a plain EVM. Monad's cold storage access is roughly four times dearer, and Monad charges the gas limit, not gas used.
  • The Arena is about 36.5 KB. That is fine on Monad (128 KB limit) and would not deploy on a chain with the standard 24 KB limit.
  • The contracts are not audited and not formally verified.
  • Records are unranked until blind matchmaking exists.
  • Full's privacy depends on unpredictable client salts. The first player to reveal with a card opening shows it before the other side decides whether to reveal. The other side can only forfeit, which is the same as in Lite.
  • The Lite reading-skill result is just under target, and the sample sizes behind the depth numbers are modest.

Run it

  • Engine tests: pnpm --filter @sublevel/engine test. Contract tests: forge test in contracts/. Chain client tests: pnpm --filter @sublevel/chain test.
  • Web client: pnpm --filter web dev. Add ?r=2d for the flat view.
  • Deploy: contracts/script/Deploy.s.sol sends the single Bootstrap transaction. Use an encrypted keystore or an agent wallet session, never a pasted key.
